## Description

```
There is a machine in a small Dutch town that costs more than a jumbo jet,
is built from 100,000 parts sourced from 5,000 suppliers across 29 countries,
and ships in 250 crates.

Exactly one company on Earth knows how to build it. Without it, the most
advanced computer chips in the world do not get made — not in America, not in
China, nowhere.

This is how the most sophisticated supply chain humans have ever built ended up
balanced on two chokepoints: one machine, and one island. And why hundreds of
billions of dollars in subsidies haven't been able to widen either of them.

SOURCES & CORRECTIONS
Figures in this video come from ASML and Carl Zeiss SMT technical disclosures,
TrendForce foundry market data, AlixPartners' 2021 shortage forecast, Rhodium
Group and Bloomberg Economics blockade modelling, US BIS export control rules,
and reporting from Reuters and the Financial Times.

A note on the big numbers: the blockade and war figures are modelled scenarios,
not forecasts. Rhodium measures gross economic activity at risk; Bloomberg
models GDP loss including sanctions and escalation. They are not directly
comparable, and the video says so on screen.

Market share figures use TrendForce's pure-play foundry series (~70% for TSMC
in 2025). Trackers that measure a broader market including non-foundry services
report a lower figure (~38%). Both are defensible; they count different things.

If I got something wrong, tell me in the comments and I'll pin a correction.
```
